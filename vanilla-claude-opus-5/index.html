About the model

This is a restricted N-body simulation in the spirit of Toomre & Toomre (1972). 100,000 test stars ride in the combined gravity of two dark-matter halos: about 1.4 × 1012 M☉ for the Milky Way and 1.7 × 1012 M☉ for Andromeda, with flat rotation curves of 220 and 250 km/s.

The halos start from today’s measured values: 770 kpc apart, closing at 110 km/s, and drifting sideways at 32 km/s (measurements range from 17 to 57). Dynamical friction slowly drains their orbit until the two cores merge.

Is it certain?

The timing follows the classic scenario of van der Marel et al. (2012): first passage in about 4 billion years, then a single elliptical galaxy, nicknamed “Milkomeda,” about 2 billion years later. A 2025 study using Gaia and Hubble data (Sawala et al., Nature Astronomy) also accounts for M33 and the Large Magellanic Cloud. It puts the chance of a merger within 10 billion years at roughly 50%.

Simplifications

Stars don’t feel each other’s gravity, so the spiral arms are painted on at the start and wind up once the encounter begins. Individual stars almost never collide. Even where the two galaxies overlap, the gaps between stars are tens of millions of times wider than the stars themselves.
