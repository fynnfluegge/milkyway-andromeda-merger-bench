About the model

What you are looking at

Each galaxy is a Hernquist sphere of dark matter and stars. The two centres orbit each other as a two-body problem with Chandrasekhar dynamical friction, which is what eventually drags them together. The stars are massless test particles that respond to both galaxies but not to each other, the classic Toomre & Toomre approach. It captures tidal tails, bridges and the final coalescence, but not gas, star formation or the fine structure a full self-gravitating simulation would show.

Timings follow the classic prediction (first close pass in about 4 billion years, merger about 2 billion years later). Newer work that folds in the Large Magellanic Cloud and the uncertainty in Andromeda's sideways motion puts the odds of a merger within 10 billion years near a coin flip, so treat this as one plausible future rather than a forecast. The merged remnant, an elliptical galaxy, is what astronomers have nicknamed Milkomeda.
